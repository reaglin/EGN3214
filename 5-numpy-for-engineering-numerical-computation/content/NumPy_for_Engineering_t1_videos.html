Introduction

NumPy is more than a container for numbers: it ships with a large library of ready-made functions that do the heavy lifting of engineering computation. These fall into a few families you will use again and again: mathematical and arithmetic functions (element-wise addition, powers, exponentials, trigonometry), statistical functions (mean, median, standard deviation, min/max, sums along an axis), linear algebra functions in numpy.linalg (matrix multiplication, determinants, inverses, solving Ax = b, eigenvalues), array manipulation functions (reshape, transpose, concatenate, stack, split), sorting and searching functions (sort, argsort, argmax, where, searchsorted), and input/output functions for loading and saving data (loadtxt, genfromtxt, save, savetxt).

This matters in EGN3214 because engineering problems usually arrive as tables of measurements or systems of equations, not as single scalars. Writing Python loops over thousands of sensor readings is slow and error-prone; calling a vectorized NumPy function on the whole array is one line, far faster, and much closer to the mathematical notation you already use in statics, circuits, and thermodynamics.

As you watch the videos below, pay attention to three things: (1) the distinction between element-wise operations (the * operator) and true matrix operations (np.dot, the @ operator, or numpy.linalg routines) — this is the single most common source of bugs; (2) how shapes and axes control what a function returns, especially for statistics computed along rows versus columns; and (3) when a problem outgrows NumPy and is better handled by a specialized library such as SciPy. Keep a notebook open and retype the examples rather than only watching.
